Data Science Toolkit

Bring a question and a dataset. Get the next best step.

The toolkit reads your data, chooses the techniques that fit your question, tells you why, and hands you a notebook for each step. When a step depends on what the last one found, you upload the result and it decides again.

  • Private: your file never leaves this browser
  • Explained: every choice shows its reason
  • Runnable: Jupyter notebooks to download

How it decides

Rules you can read, not a black box.

The toolkit's "model" is a set of written rules applied to measurements of your data. That is why it can run privately in your browser and why every recommendation can show its evidence.

The question picks the family

Predict a number: regression. Predict a category: classification. Compare groups: a hypothesis test. Find segments: clustering. Over time: forecasting. Free text: NLP.

The reader's task picks the chart

Compare sizes: sorted bars. Spread across groups: box plots. Two numbers: scatter, or density when rows pile up. Over time: a line. Shares: stacked bars. No pies, no dual axes.

The data picks the details

Skew decides median vs. mean and whether to log-transform. Class balance decides the metric. Correlated features call for regularization. Normality decides which test.

Results unlock the next step

Some choices cannot be made up front. Cleaning comes back before exploring; exploration comes back before modeling; model scores come back before tuning.

Flag, don't delete

Outliers and suspicious placeholder values are reported, never silently removed. Whether an extreme value is an error or the most important row is a human call.

Always beat a baseline

Every model is scored against a naive guess and on data it has not seen. A score with nothing to compare against says very little.

Too good is a warning

Near-perfect scores usually mean a column leaked the answer. The toolkit treats them as a problem to investigate, not a win.

Split to match reality

Random rows when rows are independent; by time when predicting forward; by group when an entity repeats. The wrong split inflates every score.

Shortlist, then compete

Row count, feature count, overlap and curvature rule each model in or out, from linear and KNN to boosting and neural networks. Survivors are cross-validated.

No number without its spread

Means carry confidence intervals and variance; scores carry fold deviation and a bootstrap interval. Models inside each other's noise are a tie, and the simpler one wins.
